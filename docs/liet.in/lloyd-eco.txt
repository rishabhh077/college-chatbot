Project Significance
Lloyd Eco Club’s comprehensive initiatives have transformed campus sustainability efforts through multiple interconnected projects that create a holistic impact:
Green Plant Belt Project
Our flagship initiative creates lush green corridors across campus, significantly improving indoor air quality, reducing pollutants, and fostering eco-consciousness among students. This hands-on project teaches responsibility, teamwork, and the tangible connection between human action and environmental restoration.
DIY Recycle Project
The EVS BAS 204 DIY Recycling initiative empowers students to transform waste into creative solutions. By promoting sustainability, creativity, and resourcefulness, this project demonstrates that environmental responsibility is not just a duty but an opportunity for innovation. Students learn that every material has potential for regeneration.
Earth Day & Environment Day Celebrations
Annual celebrations create awareness about climate action, biodiversity conservation, and sustainable living practices. These events unite the campus community around shared environmental values and inspire collective action for planetary health.
Carbon Footprint Case Study
Students engage in real-world analysis of environmental impact, pledging to reduce their carbon footprint. This project bridges theory and practice, enabling data-driven decision-making for sustainable futures.
Collectively, these projects achieve:
Improved air quality, enhanced mental well-being through green spaces, waste reduction, climate action participation, and most importantly—cultivating a generation of environmentally conscious technologists who understand that sustainability must be central to all innovation.

Green Plant Belt Project – Lloyd Institute of Engineering and Technology
1. Significance of the Project
Enhances indoor air quality and reduces pollutants.
Promotes eco-conscious behavior among students.
Creates a visually appealing and calming learning environment.
Encourages students to take ownership of sustainable practices.
Acts as a hands-on extension of environmental education.
Aligns with Green Campus Initiatives and Swachh Bharat Abhiyan goals.
Corridor project
2. Process and Implementation Plan
Step 1: Orientation & Sensitization
Conduct awareness sessions on the benefits of indoor plants for environment and mental health.
Step 2: Plant Selection
Choose low-maintenance, air-purifying indoor plants like:
Snake Plant,
Money Plant,
Spider Plant,
Peace Lily,
ZZ Plant.
Step 3: Group Formation
Students are divided into Green Squads for various campus zones.
Step 4: Setup & Plantation
Each group provides plants using eco-friendly pots; all plants are labeled with names and care instructions.
“Green Corridor – Growing a Sustainable Path for Tomorrow.”
3. Maintenance Plan
Weekly care rota by students.
Record keeping of sunlight, watering, and growth.
Monthly review by faculty or eco-club members.
Use organic compost from college waste.
4. Expected Outcomes
Greener, healthier learning environment.
Hands-on student participation in sustainability.
Improved responsibility and teamwork.
Potential research data on plant growth or air quality.
5. Community & Nursery Collaboration
Local nurseries provide plants and workshops.
Community engagement via RWA partnerships and donations.

They help relieve stress, enhance emotional well-being, and promote mindfulness. Working together on green initiatives also strengthens teamwork, communication, and environmental awareness.
Physical Activities
Planting, watering, rotating, and composting.

Mental & Emotional Benefits

Benefits of Corridor Plants
Improved Air Quality:
Removes CO₂ and indoor pollutants.
Noise Reduction:
Plants deflect sound, promoting focus.
Cooling Effect:
Natural temperature control through transpiration.
Mental Health:
Boosts mood, productivity, and reduces eye strain.
Educational Value:
Plants as tools for biology and sustainability topics.
“Where Every Corridor Breathes Green.”
Recommended Plants for Corridor
Best Plants for Corridor (All-Season, Summer-Hardy, Low Maintenance)
1. Areca Palm (Dypsis lutescens)
Air purifier gives a tropical feel.
Thrives in indirect sunlight.
Needs watering 2–3 times/week in summer.
2. Snake Plant (Sansevieria trifasciata)
Super tough and survives heat and low light.
Excellent oxygen-producing plant, even at night.
Water only once a week.
3. Spider Plant (Chlorophytum comosum)
Very easy to grow, tolerant of heat and shade.
Removes carbon monoxide and other pollutants.
Needs minimal care.
4. Aloe Vera
Medicinal plant that thrives in sun and partial shade.
Very low water requirement.
Perfect for summer, heat-tolerant.
5. Money Plant (Pothos)
rows well in water or soil.
Can climb or hang; great for corridor walls.
Needs only moderate watering and light.
6. Rubber Plant (Ficus elastica)
Attractive large leaves; good air purifier.
Can tolerate both bright and low light.
Needs weekly watering in summer.
7. ZZ Plant (Zamioculcas zamiifolia)
Survives neglect, perfect for corridors.
Glossy leaves don’t require frequent watering.
Tolerates low light and heat.
Tips for Planting
Use pots with drainage holes and trays underneath.
Rotate plants and schedule regular watering.
Use indirect sunlight and minimal water as needed.
Educational Integration
Use 5–10 minutes in class for:
Plant care and discussion.
Student “Green Talks.”
Link to topics like photosynthesis, air quality, sustainability, and waste management.

EVS BAS 204 DIY Recycle Project
Section: D/E/F
Theme: Earth Day 2024 – “Planet vs. Plastics”
Things To Do:
Hands-on activity (submit one recycled item)
Typed short report describing your activity and what you learned
Submission Due: 14th June 2024
Total Points: 20
Participation and submission are mandatory for all EVS students.
Objective
DIY recycling projects promote sustainability, encourage creativity, build social bonds, save money, and reduce environmental waste.
Purpose
The purpose of DIY (Do-It-Yourself) recycling projects spans multiple benefits across environmental, educational, economic, and creative aspects.
Outcomes
Environmental Benefits
Waste Reduction:
Helps minimize landfill waste.
Resource Conservation:
Reuses materials, conserving natural resources.
Pollution Reduction:
Less pollution compared to producing new products.
Educational Benefits
Increases awareness of environmental issues and recycling importance.
Develops skills like crafting, basic engineering, and creative problem-solving.
Links well with STEM education (Science, Tech, Engineering, Math).
Community & Social Benefits
Builds community spirit through shared eco-goals.
Teaches others via educational outreach and real-world learning.
Promotes teamwork and environmental responsibility.
Final Note
Through this project, students engage in hands-on recycling while making a positive impact on their surroundings—both socially and environmentally.
